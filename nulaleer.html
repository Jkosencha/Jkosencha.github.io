The Problem

Nula Leer sells online first, to customers in Kenya and abroad. Without a shop, every order would mean back-and-forth on WhatsApp to agree the price, collect payment and arrange delivery, all tracked by hand. The brand needed a proper shop that takes M-Pesa and card payments, and one place to run each order through to delivery, with customers kept updated without extra messages.

What I Built

  • Online shop: collections, product pages, cart and checkout, with delivery fees shown at checkout and a WhatsApp chat button.
  • Payments: customers choose M-Pesa or card (including international cards and Apple Pay) and pay on Paystack's secure page.
  • Admin dashboard: sales and best sellers at a glance, orders filtered by status and updated several at a time, an order page with its full history, products with stock and colours, and a customer list.
  • Automatic updates: customers get an email and an SMS when their order is paid and again when it ships.
  • Search and sharing: a search description and social preview tags, so the shop looks right on Google and when its link is shared.

Key Decisions

  • The server sets the price. Prices and delivery fees are worked out in serverless functions, never taken from the browser, so nobody can edit a total before paying.
  • Only a confirmed, full payment counts. Paystack confirms each payment with a signed webhook, and an order is marked paid only when the amount matches exactly.
  • Card details never touch the site. Payment happens on Paystack's page, which keeps card data off the shop's servers entirely.
  • Locked down at the database. Row-level security in Supabase means only approved admins can see orders and customer details.
  • Privacy-friendly analytics. Umami counts visitors without cookies, so there is no cookie banner in the way of shopping.
